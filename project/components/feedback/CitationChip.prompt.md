Where an answer came from: a small pill under a piece of written text, naming a help page and going to it.

A link rather than a button, because a reader has every right to open the whole page rather than the sentence an assistant chose for them. It carries a page name and nothing else: no figure, no counterparty, no date, no confidence score. A citation that says 92% match invites the reader to trust the number instead of reading the page, which is the opposite of the point.

Radius 8 on grouped-light, caption-1, 26px: the dimmed quantity chip's recipe, so it reads as a label rather than as an action competing with the answer above it. Goes in `AssistantAnswer`'s `sources` slot.

```jsx
<CitationChip label="The modules, and who can open them" href="/help/modules-and-access" />
<CitationChip label="Finding your way around" href="/help/finding-your-way#the-sidebar" icon="file-text" />
```
